# Choner Language Flow: From Starting to Commitment

## Purpose

This document defines the user-facing language for the Choner journey from starting a healthy activity to forming a shared commitment with a partner.

The goal is to make the experience **welcoming, human, and emotionally engaging**, without introducing the word **“commitment”** too early.

A user is initially being invited to start something healthy with another person. The commitment becomes meaningful only after two people have found each other and agreed to do it together.

---

# 1. Empty Challenges

## Heading

**Start something together**

## Supporting text

*Pick what you want to do with your partner.*

## Button

**Let’s do this**

### Purpose

This should feel like an invitation rather than a task.

Avoid:

- Create my first commitment
- Create my first challenge
- Set your commitment
- Start a 7-day challenge

The user does not need to feel that they are signing up for an obligation.

The emotional message is:

> **Let's start something together.**

---

# 2. Activity Selected

Once the user chooses an activity, move from choosing what to do toward making it real.

## Heading

**Let’s make it happen.**

## Supporting text

*Find someone who wants to do it too.*

### Purpose

The user has chosen the activity.

The next natural step is to find another person who wants to do the same thing.

The experience should feel like progress rather than another form.

---

# 3. Partner Found

Once the user finds and accepts a suitable partner:

## Heading

**You found your match. 👋**

## Supporting text

*Now let’s plan your first one.*

### Purpose

This moment should feel like a small emotional win.

The user has gone from:

> I want to do this.

to:

> I found someone who wants to do it too.

The word **“match”** is intentional because the user has just completed the matching experience.

It gives the moment a sense of discovery and achievement.

---

# 4. Both Agree

Once both people have agreed on the first activity/session:

## Heading

**You’re in. 🔥**

## Supporting text

*You’ve got something to show up for together.*

### Purpose

This is the transition from finding someone to actually having something shared.

The experience should communicate:

> You both said yes.

This is the moment when the shared relationship starts becoming real.

---

# 5. Commitment Established

Once both people have agreed and the shared plan is established, the product can introduce the formal product term:

## Heading

**Your commitment**

### Purpose

At this point, “commitment” no longer feels like a requirement imposed by the app.

It is the natural result of:

- Choosing something to do
- Finding someone who wants to do it
- Planning it together
- Agreeing to show up

The user now has a real shared commitment with another person.

---

# 6. Emotional Progression

The intended emotional progression is:

**Start something together**

↓

**Let’s make it happen**

↓

**You found your match**

↓

**You’re in**

↓

**Your commitment**

Each step should feel like a natural progression rather than a series of forms.

---

# 7. Core Language Principle

> **Don’t introduce the word “commitment” before the user has something worth committing to.**

The commitment should feel like the **natural result of meeting someone and agreeing to do something together**, not like a form the user has to fill out.

This keeps the experience welcoming while preserving the core Choner idea.

---

# 8. Why This Language Works for Choner

Choner is built around two people showing up for each other.

The early experience should therefore emphasize:

**Possibility**
→ Start something together

**Action**
→ Let’s make it happen

**Connection**
→ You found your match

**Agreement**
→ You’re in

**Commitment**
→ Your commitment

The product language should move from **emotion and possibility** toward **structure and accountability**, instead of leading with obligation.

# 10. UX Tone

The language should consistently feel:

- Welcoming
- Human
- Encouraging
- Low-pressure
- Relationship-oriented
- Simple
- Forward-moving

Avoid language that feels:

- Formal
- Clinical
- Bureaucratic
- Punitive
- Overly motivational
- Like a fitness challenge marketplace

The user should feel:

> **I want to do this.**
>
> **Someone wants to do it too.**
>
> **We're doing it together.**
>
> **Now we have something to show up for.**
