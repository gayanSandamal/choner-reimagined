## 1. Strategic positioning decision

Choner should not initially feel like another fitness tracker, workout-content app, or habit tracker.

**The attraction is meeting new people and discovering people who share your interests. The reason those people connect is to do healthy activities together. The longer-term benefit is building consistency.**

The intended emotional experience is:

1. Discover people who share an interest in healthy activities.
2. Find someone who wants to do the same activity.
3. Connect and decide how to do it together.
4. Make a shared commitment.
5. Show up and build consistency.

A concise way to express the idea:

> **Meet someone new. Find something healthy to do together.**

Choner can borrow the excitement of discovering and connecting with new people from social and matching apps, but it must not be positioned as a dating app or a general-purpose social network. The shared healthy activity is the reason people connect.

### The positioning hierarchy

- **Attraction:** Meet new people.
- **Connection:** Find someone who shares your interests.
- **Activity:** Do healthy activities together.
- **Outcome:** Build healthy habits and become more consistent.

### Core distinction

The marketing should lead with the social discovery experience, not with discipline, tracking, weight loss, or fitness performance. Healthy habits remain central, but the first emotional hook is the possibility of meeting someone new and finding an activity partner.

## 3. Product accuracy checks before publishing

The store listing must accurately describe what is available in the actual release. Do not claim a feature is available merely because it is planned or discussed.

Before finalizing the listing, verify:

- Which activities are selectable in the production build. The draft currently names running, walking, yoga, home workouts and cycling.
- Whether both participation modes are available: **Together** and **Separately, together**.
- Whether partner discovery and matching are available in the release build.
- Whether users can invite someone they already know.
- Whether users can create shared commitments, plan sessions, check in and build shared streaks.
- Whether matching is geographically limited at launch, including the selected Colombo areas.
- Whether any stated feature is still a prototype, planned feature, or not yet implemented.

If a capability is not available in the release, remove it from the store description or clearly label it as future functionality outside the store listing. The public listing should primarily describe what users can do now.

### Important: do not imply these features are live unless confirmed

The following ideas have been discussed as product directions, but should not be represented as existing features without confirmation:

- Live video companionship for home workouts or yoga.
- Live nearby search or a real-time map of available activity partners.
- Instant online activity sessions that users can join immediately.
- Group live sessions and activity-based push notifications.
- Behavioral Intelligence Engine (BIE) capabilities beyond what is implemented today.

## 4. Marketing campaign foundation

The same positioning should drive Choner's launch campaign.

### Lead with social discovery

The campaign should make people curious about meeting someone new through a shared healthy activity. The primary message is not “get fit” or “be more disciplined.” It is:

- Meet someone new.
- Find someone who shares your interests.
- Find your running partner.
- Meet someone to walk with.
- Discover people who want to do the same things you do.

Then connect that excitement to the purpose: doing healthy activities together and becoming more consistent.

### Preferred campaign concept

> **Your next workout buddy could be a stranger.**

This is the leading campaign concept to explore. It creates curiosity, makes the social discovery element clear, and gives people a reason to visit Choner.

### Other campaign lines to explore

1. **Meet someone. Find your thing. Show up together.**
2. **New people. Shared interests. Healthier habits.**
3. **You might be one activity away from meeting someone new.**
4. **Meet someone new. Do something good together.**
5. **Find someone to show up with.**

Use these as creative directions, not as five competing taglines in the same asset. Each post or video should communicate one idea.

### Creative direction

Use relatable, human situations:

- Someone who wants to start running but has trouble following through alone.
- Two strangers who both arrive at the same place looking for an activity partner.
- Someone discovering that another person shares their interest in walking, yoga, running or home workouts.
- Two people meeting for the first time and beginning an activity together.
- The excitement and anticipation of finding someone new with a shared goal.

Show ordinary people rather than only highly athletic fitness models. Choner should feel welcoming to people who are trying to become more active, including those who have struggled to build consistency.

For Sri Lankan launch content, use recognisably Sri Lankan people, settings and everyday situations. The early campaign can focus on Colombo and the locations where in-person matching is actually supported.

## 5. Brand and messaging guardrails

### Do

- Lead with meeting and connecting with new people.
- Make shared healthy activities the reason for the connection.
- Make consistency the longer-term benefit.
- Use warm, human, inviting language.
- Show people who are relatable, not only already-fit athletes.
- Make it clear that a person can invite someone they already know or discover a new activity partner, where both options are supported.
- Keep the message focused on one clear idea at a time.
- Make geographic availability and feature availability clear and accurate.

### Avoid

- Positioning Choner primarily as a fitness tracker, calorie counter, workout library or habit tracker.
- Making the campaign sound like a lecture about discipline, motivation or weight loss.
- Positioning Choner as a dating app or general social network.
- Suggesting that users can browse or contact unlimited strangers if the product only supports a structured matching flow.
- Claiming live video, live maps, instant sessions, AI coaching or other roadmap features are available before they are implemented.
- Using exaggerated promises such as guaranteed results, guaranteed matches or guaranteed habit formation.
- Using generic fitness clichés as the main brand idea.

### Messaging formula

When writing a new campaign message, use this order:

**Meet someone new → Discover a shared activity → Do it together → Build consistency.**

The opening should create curiosity about the person and the connection. The remainder should make the healthy-activity purpose clear.

## 6. Google Play compliance and listing checks

The app category is **Health & Fitness**, but category selection does not by itself determine the answers in the Play Console Health apps declaration.

Review the actual app functionality and answer the declaration accurately. In particular, check whether the app monitors or records physical activity, exercise routines or workouts. Do not assume every answer should be “No” without checking the available functionality and the wording of Google's declaration.

Also verify the current Play Console limits, metadata rules, tags and policy requirements directly in the console before submission. Choose only tags that accurately represent Choner and are available in Google's current tag list.

Use this document as the agreed strategic direction for Choner's Play Store listing and launch marketing.

When working on the listing or marketing assets:

1. Preserve the positioning hierarchy: meeting new people is the attraction; shared healthy activities are the connection; consistency is the outcome.

### Corrected, and why

**"Discover people who want to work towards similar goals and find someone to
do it with" is not what the app does.** This was the one real conflict.

`app/find/who-else.tsx` carries this comment at the top of the file:

> "Already on the move": proof that real people are here, never a candidate
> list. Read-only on purpose: a card does not open a profile, and there is no
> message or match action. No location on any of them: without it this is a
> first name and an activity, not a way to find someone in person.

And `components/find/MatchOffer.tsx` shows the actual flow: Choner offers
**one** person, with a clock on the offer, and the user confirms, declines, or
asks for another. There is no browsing, no profile to open, no way to contact
anyone you were not matched with.

This brief's own guardrails forbid the copy it drafted: *"Avoid suggesting that
users can browse or contact unlimited strangers if the product only supports a
structured matching flow."* The positioning survives intact, because meeting
someone new is still the attraction. Only the mechanism was described wrongly.

The listing copy now says: *"Choner then finds you one partner and shows you
who they are. You can accept, or pass and look again. There is no feed of
strangers to scroll, no profiles to browse, and nobody to message out of the
blue."* That is the same emotional promise, told truthfully.

### Two format problems with the draft

**Play does not render Markdown in the full description.** The draft uses `#`
for a heading, `**bold**` for section titles and `-` for bullets. All of them
would appear on the store page as literal characters. The copy in
[LISTING.md](LISTING.md) uses ALL CAPS lines and plain text, which is what the
field actually supports.

**The brand tagline uses a curly apostrophe** in "Turn 'I should' into 'I
did.'". Harmless in Play, worth knowing when it goes into an asset pipeline.

### One correction to an earlier answer of Claude's

Section 6 of this brief is right and Claude was wrong. Claude had said the
**Health apps declaration** would be "all no". Choner records sessions,
check-ins and streaks against physical activities, so at least the questions
about recording or monitoring physical activity are likely to be yes. Answer
that form against the app, question by question, not from the assumption that
a non-medical app answers no to everything. It still almost certainly carries
no medical, research or measurement obligations.
